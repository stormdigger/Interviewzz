# 💰 Offer & Negotiation

> Negotiation is not a confrontation. It's a collaborative problem: the company wants you to accept, you want to accept, and the question is what package makes that happen.

> ⚠️ Practices vary significantly by country and company. Specific numbers here are US-tech-centric; the *principles* transfer, the *conventions* may not.

## 1. The Mental Model

```
   ⭐ THE THREE FACTS THAT SHOULD CALM YOU DOWN

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ① ⭐ THEY HAVE ALREADY DECIDED THEY WANT YOU.                 │
   │    An offer means you beat every other candidate. Replacing  │
   │    you means restarting a process that cost them weeks and   │
   │    a great deal of money. ⭐ Your leverage is highest at this │
   │    exact moment and it never returns.                        │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ② ⭐ NEGOTIATING IS EXPECTED, AND NORMAL.                     │
   │    Recruiters negotiate constantly. It is a routine part of  │
   │    their job. ⚠️ The fear that a polite negotiation will      │
   │    cause an offer to be rescinded is essentially unfounded   │
   │    in normal circumstances.                                  │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ③ ⭐ THE FIRST OFFER IS RARELY THE MAXIMUM.                   │
   │    Most companies leave room deliberately. Accepting         │
   │    immediately leaves that room on the table permanently.    │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
   ⭐ WHY IT COMPOUNDS MORE THAN PEOPLE REALIZE

   A $15,000 difference in starting salary isn't $15,000.

   • Future raises are usually a PERCENTAGE of your current base
   • Your next job's offer is often anchored on this one
   • Bonuses are typically a percentage of base
   • ⭐ Over ten years, compounding at a few percent, that
     $15,000 becomes well over $200,000

   ⭐ A thirty-minute conversation is the highest hourly-rate
     work you will ever do.
```

```
   ⭐ THE FRAME THAT WORKS: COLLABORATIVE, NOT ADVERSARIAL

   ❌ "I demand more money."
   ✅ "I'm genuinely excited about this role. There's a gap
      between the offer and what I'd need to sign immediately —
      can we work on closing it?"

   ⭐ You are on the SAME SIDE. They want you to say yes. Frame
     every request as removing an obstacle to that.
```

## 2. Understanding Compensation

```
   ⭐ THE COMPONENTS — and their very different risk profiles

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ BASE SALARY      ⭐ Guaranteed cash. The most reliable        │
   │                  component and the one that drives raises,   │
   │                  bonuses, and loan applications.             │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ SIGNING BONUS    ⭐ One-time. Easiest thing to negotiate,     │
   │                  because it doesn't affect internal salary   │
   │                  bands or set a precedent.                   │
   │                  ⚠️ Usually has a clawback if you leave       │
   │                    within 12-24 months.                      │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ANNUAL BONUS     Target percentage of base, ⚠️ dependent on   │
   │                  company and individual performance.         │
   │                  ⭐ Ask what percentage of target was         │
   │                    actually paid the last two years.         │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ⭐ EQUITY         The largest and most variable component at  │
   │                  senior levels. See §3 — it deserves its own │
   │                  section because most people misvalue it.    │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ BENEFITS         Retirement match · health insurance ·       │
   │                  ⭐ 401k match is free money and often        │
   │                    ignored in comparisons                    │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
   ⚠️ "TOTAL COMPENSATION" IS A MISLEADING SINGLE NUMBER

   $200k base + $100k equity ≠ $200k base + $100k bonus
   ≠ $300k base.

   They have completely different risk, liquidity, and tax
   profiles. ⭐ Always break an offer into its components before
   comparing.
```

```
   ⭐ VESTING SCHEDULES — read this carefully

   STANDARD (4 years, 1-year cliff)
     Year 1: ⚠️ NOTHING until the 12-month cliff, then 25% at once
     Years 2-4: the rest, usually monthly or quarterly

   ⭐ FRONT-LOADED (Amazon is the well-known example)
     5% / 15% / 40% / 40%
     ⚠️ Years 1-2 are deliberately light, offset by a large
       signing bonus that also front-loads. ⭐ Your year-3 income
       can DROP sharply unless refreshers make up the gap.
       Model all four years, not year one.

   ⭐ BACK-LOADED or with a longer cliff — read the actual grant
     document, not the offer summary.
```

## 3. Equity Explained

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ⭐ RSUs — Restricted Stock Units (public companies)           │
   │   You receive SHARES as they vest. They have value as long   │
   │   as the stock has value.                                    │
   │   ⭐ Taxed as ORDINARY INCOME at vest, on the value that day. │
   │   ✅ Simple, and worth something even if the stock falls.     │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ⚠️ OPTIONS (ISOs / NSOs) — mostly private companies           │
   │   The RIGHT to BUY shares at a fixed STRIKE price.           │
   │   ⭐ Worth nothing unless the company's value exceeds the     │
   │     strike. ⚠️ You must PAY to exercise, and you may owe tax  │
   │     on the paper gain (AMT for ISOs) on stock you cannot     │
   │     sell.                                                    │
   │   ⚠️⚠️ Typically a 90-DAY EXERCISE WINDOW after you leave —   │
   │     if you can't afford to exercise, you lose everything.    │
   │     ⭐ Some companies offer 10-year windows. ASK.             │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
   ⭐⭐ VALUING PRIVATE-COMPANY EQUITY — the questions to ask

   ⚠️ "0.5% of the company" is not a number you can evaluate.
     Ask:

   □ ⭐ How many shares TOTAL are outstanding, fully diluted?
     (Percentage without this is meaningless)
   □ What was the most recent 409A valuation and preferred
     price?
   □ ⭐ What's the strike price, and what's my exercise cost in
     total dollars?
   □ ⭐ What's the LIQUIDATION PREFERENCE stack?
     (⚠️ If investors have 2× participating preferred, common
      shareholders can receive nothing in a modest exit even
      when it looks like a success)
   □ How much has been raised, and at what valuations?
   □ ⭐ How long is the post-termination exercise window?
   □ Is there a secondary market or tender offer history?
   □ Runway and burn rate?

   ⭐ THE HONEST FRAMING: most startup equity is worth zero.
     A minority is life-changing. Treat it as a lottery ticket
     with positive expected value, not as compensation you're
     counting on. ⚠️ Never accept a below-market salary purely
     for equity unless you can genuinely afford that outcome.
```

```
   ⭐ REFRESHERS AND THE FOUR-YEAR CLIFF

   ⚠️ Your initial grant vests over four years and then STOPS.
     If refresh grants don't fill the gap, your compensation
     drops sharply in year five.

   ⭐ ASK EXPLICITLY: "What does a typical refresh grant look
     like for someone performing well at this level?"

   ⭐ Also ask about the grant DATE convention — equity granted
     at a fixed dollar value converts to shares at the price on
     the grant date, so the timing materially affects what you
     receive.
```

## 4. Levelling

```
   ⭐⭐ LEVEL MATTERS MORE THAN NEGOTIATION.

   Moving up one level typically changes compensation by
   20-50%. Negotiating hard within a level might move it 10-15%.

   ⭐ SO: negotiate the LEVEL FIRST, and the numbers second.
     And level is far easier to influence BEFORE the offer is
     issued than after.
```

```
   ⭐ ROUGH INDUSTRY MAPPING (varies by company)

   ┌──────────────┬────────┬────────┬──────────┬────────────────┐
   │ Generic      │ Google │ Meta   │ Amazon   │ Microsoft      │
   ├──────────────┼────────┼────────┼──────────┼────────────────┤
   │ Junior       │ L3     │ E3     │ SDE I    │ 59-60          │
   │ Mid          │ L4     │ E4     │ SDE II   │ 61-62          │
   │ ⭐ Senior     │ L5     │ E5     │ SDE III  │ 63-64          │
   │ Staff        │ L6     │ E6     │ Principal│ 65-66          │
   │ Senior Staff │ L7     │ E7     │ Sr Princ │ 67-68          │
   └──────────────┴────────┴────────┴──────────┴────────────────┘

   ⭐ Levels.fyi and Blind have real reported data by level,
     company, and location. Use them — the information
     asymmetry in negotiation is the main thing working
     against you, and it's now largely solvable.
```

```
   ⭐ IF YOU'RE LEVELLED LOWER THAN EXPECTED

   Ask directly and without defensiveness:
     "I was expecting to be considered at the senior level
      given [specific scope: I've led projects across three
      teams, I own the on-call rotation, I've mentored four
      engineers]. Can you help me understand the levelling
      decision, and is a re-evaluation possible?"

   ⭐ Sometimes there's a formal down-levelling appeal or a
     re-interview. It's worth asking, because the difference
     compounds for years.

   ⚠️ But be realistic: if the interview signal genuinely didn't
     support the level, pushing hard damages the relationship.
     ⭐ An alternative is accepting the level with an explicit,
     documented conversation about the path to promotion.
```

## 5. Before the Offer

```
   ⭐ THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT PRE-OFFER MOVE:
     DON'T NAME A NUMBER FIRST.

   Whoever anchors first constrains the range. ⚠️ If you name a
   number below what they'd have offered, you've just cost
   yourself the difference, permanently.
```

```
   ⭐ DEFLECTION SCRIPTS — use whichever fits

   "I'd rather focus on whether this is the right fit first.
    I'm confident we can align on compensation if it is."

   "I don't have a specific number in mind — I'm evaluating
    the whole opportunity. What range has been budgeted for
    this role?"

   ⭐ "What does the range look like for this level? I want to
    make sure we're in the same ballpark before we both invest
    more time."

   ⭐ MANY JURISDICTIONS NOW REQUIRE DISCLOSURE ON REQUEST
     (California, Colorado, New York, Washington, and much of
     the EU under the Pay Transparency Directive). ⭐ Just ask —
     in many places they must tell you.
```

```
   ⚠️ IF PRESSED HARD FOR A NUMBER

   Give a RANGE, informed by research, anchored at the level
   you want:

   "Based on what I've seen for senior roles in this market,
    I'd expect total compensation somewhere in the $X to $Y
    range, though it depends on the equity structure and the
    specific scope."

   ⭐ Set the BOTTOM of your range at your target, not your
     minimum — they will hear the bottom.
```

```
   ⚠️ NEVER DISCLOSE YOUR CURRENT SALARY

   • ⭐ It's illegal for employers to ask in many US states and
     across much of the EU
   • Your current salary is irrelevant to your value in a new
     role
   • ⚠️ Disclosing it anchors the entire negotiation to your
     past, especially damaging if you're currently underpaid

   ⭐ DEFLECTION: "I'd prefer to focus on the market rate for
     this role and the value I'd bring, rather than my current
     compensation."
```

```
   ⭐ RESEARCH BEFORE ANY CONVERSATION
     □ Levels.fyi for the company, level, and location
     □ Blind for recent, unfiltered data points
     □ Glassdoor as a rough sanity check
     □ ⭐ Know: base range, equity range, and typical signing
       bonus at this level
     □ ⭐ Decide your NUMBER: the figure you'd accept
       immediately without hesitation
     □ And your WALK-AWAY number
```

## 6. When the Offer Arrives

```
   ⭐ THE FIRST RESPONSE — never accept on the call

   ⚠️ Even if it's excellent. Accepting instantly forfeits
     everything, and enthusiasm costs you nothing later.

   ⭐ SAY:
     "Thank you — I'm really pleased. Could you send the
      details in writing so I can review the full package
      properly? I'd like to come back to you within a few days."

   ⭐ THEN:
     □ Express genuine enthusiasm (⭐ this matters — they need
       to believe you'll accept)
     □ Ask for the FULL written breakdown
     □ Ask about anything unclear: vesting schedule, bonus
       target, refresh policy, start date flexibility
     □ Take at least 24 hours before responding substantively
```

```
   ⭐ WHAT TO ASK FOR IN WRITING
     □ Base salary
     □ Signing bonus, ⭐ and any clawback terms
     □ Equity: dollar value AND share count, vesting schedule,
       grant date convention
     □ Annual bonus target, ⭐ and actual payout history
     □ Level and title
     □ Start date
     □ ⭐ The refresh grant policy
     □ Relocation, if relevant
```

```
   ⭐ ON EXPLODING OFFERS

   ⚠️ "This expires in 48 hours" is a pressure tactic and it's
     usually soft.

   ⭐ RESPOND CALMLY:
     "I understand you'd like a decision quickly. I'm taking
      this seriously and want to give it proper consideration.
      Could we extend to [date]? I'd rather make a decision
      I'm fully confident in."

   ⭐ Most deadlines move. And a company that genuinely refuses
     any reasonable extension has told you something useful
     about how they treat people.
```

## 7. The Negotiation

```
   ⭐ THE STRUCTURE OF A NEGOTIATION CONVERSATION

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ① ⭐ ENTHUSIASM FIRST                                         │
   │    Genuinely restate why you want the role. They need to     │
   │    believe you'll sign.                                      │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ② ⭐ STATE THE GAP, NOT A DEMAND                              │
   │    "There's a gap between this and what I'd need to sign     │
   │     immediately."                                            │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ③ ⭐ GIVE A SPECIFIC NUMBER, WITH JUSTIFICATION               │
   │    Vague requests get vague responses. Anchor concretely.    │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ④ ⭐ MAKE IT EASY TO SAY YES                                  │
   │    "If you can get to X, I'll sign today."                   │
   │    ⭐ This is the single most effective sentence in the       │
   │      whole conversation — it converts a request into a       │
   │      closable deal.                                          │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ⑤ ⭐ THEN BE QUIET AND LET THEM RESPOND                       │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
   ⭐ JUSTIFICATIONS THAT ACTUALLY WORK

   ✅ ⭐ A COMPETING OFFER (strongest by a wide margin)
   ✅ ⭐ MARKET DATA: "Levels.fyi shows this level in this
      market typically at $X-Y"
   ✅ SCOPE: "I'd be taking on the on-call rotation and
      mentoring, which is beyond the base expectation"
   ✅ SPECIFIC RELEVANT EXPERIENCE the role needs
   ✅ WHAT YOU'RE LEAVING BEHIND: "I'm walking away from
      unvested equity worth $X"

   ⚠️ JUSTIFICATIONS THAT DON'T WORK
   ❌ Personal financial needs — mortgage, cost of living,
      family expenses. ⭐ Sympathetic, but not relevant to your
      value and it weakens your position.
   ❌ "I deserve it"
   ❌ Vague dissatisfaction
```

```
   ⭐ NEGOTIATE IN THIS ORDER — easiest to hardest

   1. ⭐ SIGNING BONUS — the easiest yes. One-time, no effect
      on salary bands or internal equity.
   2. EQUITY — often more flexible than base, especially at
      companies where equity is the main lever
   3. BASE SALARY — ⚠️ hardest, because it's constrained by
      internal bands and creates comparison problems
   4. ⭐ LEVEL — biggest impact, but the hardest to change
      after an offer is issued

   ⭐ If they say base is fixed, that's often genuinely true
     within a band — pivot immediately to signing bonus and
     equity rather than pushing on base.
```

```
   ⚠️ HOW MANY ROUNDS?

   ⭐ Typically 1-2 rounds of counter. Three or more starts to
     read as bad faith and damages goodwill you'll need on
     day one.

   ⭐ Make your counter count: ask once, clearly, with
     justification, and with a stated path to yes.
```

## 8. Scripts

```
   ⭐ THE STANDARD COUNTER (email or call)

   "Thank you for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about the
    role and the team, and [specific thing about the work].

    I've reviewed the package carefully. Based on my research
    for this level in this market, and given [specific
    justification], I was hoping we could get closer to
    $X base with $Y in equity.

    ⭐ If we can reach that, I'm ready to sign and would be
    delighted to join. Is there flexibility there?"
```

```
   ⭐ WITH A COMPETING OFFER

   "I want to be straightforward with you — I have another
    offer at $X total compensation. ⭐ Your role is genuinely my
    preference because [specific, credible reason].

    If you can get closer to that number, I'd accept
    immediately. Is that something you can work with?"

   ⚠️ ONLY SAY THIS IF IT'S TRUE. Recruiters occasionally
     verify, and being caught is unrecoverable.
```

```
   ⭐ WHEN THEY SAY "THIS IS OUR BEST OFFER"

   "I appreciate you being direct. Let me ask about a couple
    of other components — is there any flexibility on the
    signing bonus, or on the equity grant? Even a smaller
    adjustment there would help me get comfortable."

   ⭐ Pivoting to a different component after a "no" on base
     works surprisingly often, because the constraint is
     usually specific to base rather than to total cost.
```

```
   ⭐ WHEN YOU HAVE NO LEVERAGE (no competing offer)

   You still have some — they chose you.

   "I'm very interested in this role. I've done some research
    on compensation for this level and the offer is somewhat
    below what I'd expected. I don't have a competing offer,
    I'd simply like to make sure the package reflects the
    market rate. Is there room to move on the signing bonus
    or equity?"

   ⭐ Honesty about not having a competing offer is fine and
     often disarming. A modest, well-justified ask is usually
     met with something.
```

```
   ⭐ ASKING FOR MORE TIME

   "I'm taking this very seriously and want to make the right
    decision for both of us. I have one more conversation to
    complete this week — could I come back to you by [date]?"
```

```
   ⭐ DECLINING GRACEFULLY (⭐ do this well — it's a small industry)

   "Thank you for the offer and for the time everyone invested.
    After careful consideration I've decided to accept another
    role that's a better fit for [specific, non-insulting
    reason]. I really enjoyed meeting the team, and I hope our
    paths cross again."

   ⭐ Never burn a bridge. Recruiters move companies, and
     hiring managers remember people who declined graciously.
```

## 9. Competing Offers

```
   ⭐ A COMPETING OFFER IS THE STRONGEST LEVERAGE THERE IS.
     It transforms an abstract request into a concrete
     alternative.

   ⭐ HOW TO GENERATE ONE
     □ ⭐ Interview with several companies SIMULTANEOUSLY, not
       sequentially — this is the whole game
     □ Time the processes to converge (⭐ slow down the fast
       ones, accelerate the slow ones)
     □ ⭐ "I'm in final stages elsewhere and would like to
       align timelines" is a completely normal and effective
       request
```

```
   ⭐ USING IT WELL

   ✅ DO
   □ Be honest about the actual numbers
   □ ⭐ Express a genuine preference for THIS role, and say why
     specifically — otherwise it reads as pure leverage and
     they may let you go
   □ Give them a concrete path: "if you can reach X, I'll sign"

   ⚠️ DON'T
   □ ⚠️ Fabricate an offer — some recruiters ask for
     verification, and being caught ends the process
   □ Play companies against each other repeatedly across many
     rounds — it becomes exhausting and adversarial
   □ ⚠️ Use an offer you'd never actually accept as leverage —
     if they call your bluff you have nothing
```

```
   ⚠️ THE RISK WORTH NAMING

   Some companies explicitly do not match competing offers as
   a matter of policy. ⭐ If they say so, believe them and
   decide on the merits of what's actually on the table.
```

## 10. What's Negotiable Beyond Salary

```
   ⭐ FREQUENTLY OVERLOOKED, AND OFTEN EASIER TO GET

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ⭐ SIGNING BONUS        the easiest yes                       │
   │ ⭐ EQUITY REFRESH        ask about the policy up front        │
   │ START DATE             ⭐ extra time off between jobs is      │
   │                        genuinely valuable and costs them     │
   │                        almost nothing                        │
   │ ⭐ REMOTE / HYBRID       increasingly negotiable and highly   │
   │                        valuable                              │
   │ RELOCATION             lump sum vs managed move              │
   │ ⭐ EARLY REVIEW          "a compensation review at 6 months   │
   │                        rather than 12" — good compromise     │
   │                        when the band is genuinely capped     │
   │ TITLE                  ⭐ matters for your NEXT job's         │
   │                        levelling                             │
   │ ⭐ VISA / IMMIGRATION    sponsorship, timeline, legal costs   │
   │ LEARNING BUDGET        conferences, courses, certifications  │
   │ EQUIPMENT              hardware, home office allowance       │
   │ ⭐ VACATION              where it isn't unlimited             │
   │ ⭐ EXERCISE WINDOW       for options — asking for a 10-year   │
   │                        post-termination window is legitimate │
   │                        and occasionally granted              │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   ⭐ THE PRINCIPLE: if base is genuinely capped by a band,
     almost everything else has more flexibility. Ask what IS
     flexible rather than pushing on what isn't.
```

## 11. Evaluating an Offer

```
   ⚠️ COMPENSATION IS NOT THE ONLY VARIABLE, AND OFTEN NOT THE
     MOST IMPORTANT ONE.

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ⭐ THE WORK        Will you be doing something you find       │
   │                   interesting, at a scale that teaches you   │
   │                   something?                                 │
   │ ⭐ THE MANAGER     ⭐ The single largest predictor of job      │
   │                   satisfaction. A great role under a bad     │
   │                   manager is a bad job.                      │
   │ THE TEAM          Would you learn from these people?         │
   │ ⭐ GROWTH          Is there a credible path, and evidence     │
   │                   people actually follow it?                 │
   │ COMPANY TRAJECTORY  Growing, stable, or shrinking?           │
   │ ⭐ ENGINEERING     Deploy frequency, test culture, on-call    │
   │   CULTURE         load, technical debt tolerance             │
   │ STABILITY         Runway, profitability, recent layoffs      │
   │ ⭐ LIFE FIT        Commute, flexibility, on-call burden,      │
   │                   travel expectations                        │
   │ COMPENSATION      ⭐ Important — but it's one row in this     │
   │                   table, not the table                       │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```
   ⭐ DUE DILIGENCE QUESTIONS BEFORE SIGNING

   □ ⭐ Ask to speak with 1-2 potential teammates (⭐ a refusal
     is itself a signal)
   □ What's the on-call rotation and page volume actually like?
   □ ⭐ What happened to the last person in this role?
   □ How many people have left the team in the last year?
   □ ⭐ What's the deploy frequency and lead time?
   □ For startups: runway, burn, last raise, path to
     profitability
   □ ⭐ Check Glassdoor and Blind for recent patterns, not
     individual complaints
```

```
   ⭐ THE COMPARISON SPREADSHEET

   Build one. Rows are the factors above; columns are the
   offers. Score each 1-5 and WEIGHT them by what you actually
   care about.

   ⭐ Total comp should be broken into base / bonus / equity
     with a realistic equity valuation — not the company's
     optimistic one.

   ⭐ Doing this converts a stressful emotional decision into a
     legible one, and it frequently reveals that the
     higher-paying offer isn't the better one.
```

## 12. Common Mistakes

```
   ❌ ⭐ ACCEPTING ON THE SPOT
      Forfeits all leverage, permanently.
      ⭐ FIX: always ask for it in writing and take 24+ hours.

   ❌ ⭐ NAMING A NUMBER FIRST
      Anchors the range against you.
      ⭐ FIX: deflect, ask for their range, and remember many
        jurisdictions require disclosure on request.

   ❌ ⚠️ DISCLOSING CURRENT SALARY
      Anchors to your past rather than your value.
      ⭐ FIX: decline politely — it's illegal to ask in many
        places.

   ❌ NOT NEGOTIATING AT ALL
      The most common and most expensive mistake.
      ⭐ FIX: a polite, well-justified ask essentially never
        costs you an offer.

   ❌ ⚠️ NEGOTIATING WITHOUT RESEARCH
      Asking for a number that's absurdly high or leaving money
      on the table.
      ⭐ FIX: Levels.fyi and Blind before any conversation.

   ❌ ⚠️ MAKING IT ADVERSARIAL
      You'll work with these people from day one.
      ⭐ FIX: enthusiasm first, collaborative framing throughout.

   ❌ ⚠️ FABRICATING A COMPETING OFFER
      Occasionally verified; unrecoverable if caught.
      ⭐ FIX: only use real offers.

   ❌ ⭐ OPTIMIZING ONLY FOR MONEY
      The manager and the work determine whether you're happy,
      and whether you grow — which determines your compensation
      two jobs from now.

   ❌ ⚠️ NOT GETTING IT IN WRITING
      Verbal promises about promotion timelines, refresh
      grants, or scope evaporate when the person who made them
      changes role.
      ⭐ FIX: if it matters, it's in the offer letter.

   ❌ ⚠️ RESIGNING BEFORE THE OFFER IS SIGNED AND BACKGROUND
      CHECKS ARE CLEARED
      ⭐ FIX: signed contract first. Always.
```
